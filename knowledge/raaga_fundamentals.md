## Saptak (Octave) System

Three octaves (saptaks):
- **Mandra Saptak**: Lower octave (prefixed with a dot below or `'` before)
- **Madhya Saptak**: Middle octave (default, no marker)
- **Taar Saptak**: Upper octave (prefixed with a dot above or `'` after)

## The Seven Swaras

| Swara | Full Name     | Western Equivalent |
|-------|---------------|--------------------|
| Sa    | Shadja        | C (tonic)          |
| Re    | Rishabh       | D                  |
| Ga    | Gandhar       | E                  |
| Ma    | Madhyam       | F                  |
| Pa    | Pancham       | G                  |
| Dha   | Dhaivat       | A                  |
| Ni    | Nishad        | B                  |

**Fixed swaras**: Sa and Pa are always shuddha (never altered).
**Variable swaras**: Re, Ga, Ma, Dha, Ni have variants.

## Komal, Shuddha, Tivra

| Variant  | Notation | Applied To     | Meaning      |
|----------|----------|----------------|--------------|
| Komal    | lowercase (r, g, d, n) | Re, Ga, Dha, Ni | Flat (lower) |
| Shuddha  | uppercase (R, G, D, N) | Re, Ga, Dha, Ni | Natural      |
| Tivra    | M (uppercase)          | Ma only          | Sharp (higher)|
| Shuddha  | m (lowercase)          | Ma only          | Natural      |

Note: Ma is reversed from others — uppercase M = tivra, lowercase m = shuddha.

## The 10 Thaats (Bhatkhande System)

A Thaat is a parent scale from which raagas are derived. Every raaga belongs to one thaat.

| #  | Thaat    | Swaras             | Defining Feature              |
|----|----------|-------------------|------------------------------ |
| 1  | Bilawal  | S R G m P D N     | All shuddha (natural major)    |
| 2  | Kalyan   | S R G M P D N     | Tivra Ma                       |
| 3  | Khamaj   | S R G m P D n     | Komal Ni                       |
| 4  | Bhairav  | S r G m P d N     | Komal Re + Komal Dha           |
| 5  | Purvi    | S r G M P d N     | Komal Re + Tivra Ma + Komal Dha|
| 6  | Marwa    | S r G M P D N     | Komal Re + Tivra Ma            |
| 7  | Kafi     | S R g m P D n     | Komal Ga + Komal Ni            |
| 8  | Asavari  | S R g m P d n     | Komal Ga + Komal Dha + Komal Ni|
| 9  | Todi     | S r g M P d N     | Komal Re + Komal Ga + Tivra Ma + Komal Dha |
| 10 | Bhairavi | S r g m P d n     | All komal                      |

## Raaga Anatomy

Every raaga has these defining elements:

- **Aaroh (Arohanam)**: Ascending swara sequence — the path from Sa to upper Sa
- **Avaroh (Avarohanam)**: Descending swara sequence — the path from upper Sa back to Sa
- **Pakad**: The "signature phrase" — a short motif that instantly identifies the raaga
- **Vadi**: The "King note" — most important/dominant swara, used most frequently
- **Samvadi**: The "Queen note" — second most important, typically a 4th or 5th from Vadi
- **Chalan**: Movement rules — specific ways swaras must be approached or avoided
- **Varjit swaras**: Omitted notes (if any)
- **Anya swaras**: Alien notes occasionally used for ornamentation

## Jaati Classification

Raagas are classified by the number of swaras used:

| Jaati      | Notes in Aaroh | Notes in Avaroh | Example          |
|------------|---------------|-----------------|------------------|
| Audav      | 5             | 5               | Bhupali (5-5)    |
| Shadav     | 6             | 6               | Marwa (6-6)      |
| Sampurna   | 7             | 7               | Yaman (7-7)      |
| Audav-Sampurna | 5         | 7               | Malkauns type    |
| Sampurna-Shadav | 7        | 6               | Various          |

Mixed jaatis are common — aaroh and avaroh can have different counts.

## Time Theory (Prahar / Samay)

Traditional performance times are divided into 8 prahars of ~3 hours each:

| Prahar | Time           | Type         | Example Raagas          |
|--------|----------------|--------------|-------------------------|
| 1      | 6 AM – 9 AM    | Morning      | Bhairav, Bilawal, Todi  |
| 2      | 9 AM – 12 PM   | Late morning | Asavari, Jaunpuri       |
| 3      | 12 PM – 3 PM   | Afternoon    | Sarang, Brindavani      |
| 4      | 3 PM – 6 PM    | Late afternoon| Multani, Patdeep       |
| 5      | 6 PM – 9 PM    | Evening      | Yaman, Puriya, Marwa    |
| 6      | 9 PM – 12 AM   | Night        | Khamaj, Bageshri, Des   |
| 7      | 12 AM – 3 AM   | Late night   | Malkauns, Darbari       |
| 8      | 3 AM – 6 AM    | Pre-dawn     | Lalit, Bhatiyar         |

**Sandhi Prakash raagas**: Sung at sunrise/sunset transitions (Bhairav, Marwa, Purvi family).

**Key Rules**:
- Komal Re + Komal Dha → morning/twilight raagas
- Shuddha Re + Shuddha Dha → afternoon/evening raagas
- Tivra Ma → tends toward evening/night
- Komal Ga → often second half of day

## Vadi and Samvadi

- **Vadi** is the swara that dominates the raaga — appears most, often starts/ends phrases
- **Samvadi** supports the Vadi — typically related by a 4th (Ma-Sa) or 5th (Pa-Sa) interval
- Together they define the "gravitational center" of the raaga
- Vadi determines performance time:
  - If Vadi is in Purvanga (Sa-Ma range) → usually first half of day
  - If Vadi is in Uttaranga (Pa-Sa' range) → usually second half of day
